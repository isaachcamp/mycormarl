# Phosphate uptake animation

The standalone Manim scene is `scripts/manim_phosphate_uptake.py`, class
`PhosphateUptake`. Its numerical experiment is
`scripts/phosphate_uptake_video_data.py`; it does not change production physics.

**Current version:** isolated sparse uptake with a prescribed 2-day far-field
concentration half-life, by a 1-mm cylinder scaled ×10 to equivalent 1-cm
uptake and shifted to contact days 0/6/12/18. This is illustrative forcing,
not a mass-conserving phosphate depletion simulation.
The local view has no soil/grid backdrop. The new colony appendix adds
equal-area rings and uses the full mass-conserving soil kernels, with no
imposed P decay. See the latest dated working note below for
the equations, assumptions and validation; older experiments are historical.

## Story

1. An illustrative branched hypha grows against the plain dark background.
   The revised opening takes its visual cue from the supplied
   `r-irregularis-growth-wave-crop.mp4`: long, gently curving runners fan
   outward, with irregular lateral branches and finer secondary twigs.
   Growth reveals each filament from its attachment point; laterals wait
   until the parent has reached that junction. This is visual inspiration,
   not a measured reconstruction or a calibrated fungal growth model.
2. The surrounding colony fades away as the camera pans and
   zooms fully to one tip. The focal hypha stays visible and straightens
   continuously into the horizontal close-up.
   A single cylindrical silhouette with a semicircular head starts at the
   centre and grows rightwards, exiting the close-up before the pullback.
   After an isolated-tip hold, a live graph
   appears above and to the left, anchored to the screen. It shows the
   equivalent 1-cm uptake, falling as radial depletion develops around the
   isolated cylinder. The simulation clock begins here; the opening topology reveal
   is a schematic introduction, not a simulated growth history.
3. The camera pulls back and follows the same single, unbranched hypha. Four plots,
   labelled A–D to match spatial markers, remain in the frame. A is present
   initially; B–D are first contacted at 6, 12, and 18 days. Their histories share the same 0–20-day
   axis. Each curve is an identical local-age experiment shifted to contact;
   newer segments absorb more per length than depleted older segments.

## Historical physics — superseded continuous-grid experiment

The following block documents the preserved previous version, not the current
animation/data. Current sparse-only physics is specified in the final dated note.

The experiment uses a 16×16 axisymmetric finite-volume grid: radial extent
0–0.32 cm, depth 0–0.32 cm, and uniform radial/depth spacing 0.02 cm.
The colony is a hemisphere with radius `R(t) = 0.02 + 0.01 t` cm, with time
in days. Thus radial speed is fixed, not total biomass/length growth.
The existing `axisymmetric_hemisphere_density` function gives occupied-volume
fractions times saturation density in every partially crossed cell. Geometry
and uptake coefficients are recomputed each step at its midpoint. The first
cell is initially partly occupied, so A is labelled "present at day 0".
Graphs sample the top depth layer at radial indices 0, 4, 7, and 10;
they are annular cell averages, not resolved single-tip flux measurements.
The opening tile is illustrative; it is not the entire simulated domain.
Branch shapes, lengths on screen, diameter, and advance speed are illustrative.
Growth is prescribed continuously, rather than generated by a biomass or
policy rollout. Rendering stretches distances for legibility; no physical
distance is inferred from screen pixels. The prior abrupt full-cell experiment
and its video were preserved as `stepwise-data.json` and
`phosphate-uptake-stepwise.mp4` before replacement.

The production functions used are:

- `axisymmetric_diffusion_conductances` and `diffuse_labile_amount`: conservative
  exchange between neighbouring cells, with closed external boundaries;
- `solution_concentration_to_labile_amount` and its inverse: instantaneous
  buffering, `M = C V (theta_water + b_p)`;
- `uptake_geometry_coefficients`: sparse resistance and continuous weight
  recomputed from current partial-cell density at the fixed production
  reference time (1 day);
- `blended_uptake_transaction`: Michaelis–Menten surface kinetics, sparse and
  continuous closure blending, and an inventory cap on accepted uptake.

Fungal radius, density, kinetics, buffering, diffusion, impedance, and initial
solution P use current `FungusTraits()` and `EnvConfig()` defaults. Initial
solution P is 1 µM, hyphal radius 0.0005 cm, saturated density 2000 cm cm⁻³.
No plant competes for P. There is no external replenishment. The fixed closure
reference time is **not** replaced by segment age, as required by ADR-0004 and
the distinction recorded in ADR-0013. Arrival times label first contact only;
they do not switch cells to full density or reset concentrations or diffusion.
Resistance and blend weight evolve because density changes, not segment age.

Each plotted rate is

`q = accepted_fungal_P / (step_days * hyphal_density * cell_volume) * 1000`

in nmol P cm⁻¹ absorber d⁻¹. The denominator is the **current represented
length**, `density(t_midpoint) * cell_volume`, not eventual saturated length.
Absent cells have zero rate, with safe zero-length division. Rates are numerical
step averages plotted at step start; geometry is sampled at the step midpoint,
and inventories are recorded at step end. Before occupation, q is zero. Blue dots
in the opening are schematic soil phosphate, not individually resolved
molecules. The isolated-tip and following single-hypha views omit the soil
grid, dots, and moving uptake cues to keep the geometry isolated. All
living occupied cells continue absorbing. Greater front uptake is a result
of the displayed finite-inventory scenario, not a hard-coded tip-only sink or
a universal property of all initial conditions.

The experiment runs for 24 days at 0.01-day steps; the video follows the clock
to day 19.5. The complete 24-day data and parameter provenance are exported to
`media/phosphate-uptake/data.json` by the scene, or by the data script's CLI.
Format version 2 stores cellwise arrays as `(time, radius, depth)` and length
as a time-dependent field. Radius/depth edges, midpoint times, density fields,
initial geometry, plot indices, and growth parameters provide provenance.
`continuous-growth-data.npz` is a compressed copy of all numerical arrays.
